What Is Lorem Ipsum and Why Designers Still Use Placeholder Text

Quick answer

Where the strange Latin comes from, why designers fill layouts with it, and when placeholder text helps versus when it hurts.

By 123MiniApps · Published 2026-07-30 · Updated 2026-09-01 · 1060 words · about 5 minute read

If you have ever opened a design mockup and seen paragraphs beginning "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet," you have met the most famous placeholder text in the world. Lorem ipsum is scrambled, meaningless Latin used to fill a layout with realistic-looking text before the real copy exists. It lets designers judge how a page looks and flows without being distracted by what the words actually say.

This article explains where lorem ipsum comes from, why it is still used decades into the digital age, and, just as importantly, when you should not use it. You can generate as much as you need with the free Lorem Ipsum Generator.

Where the strange Latin actually comes from

Lorem ipsum is not random gibberish invented by a designer. It is a scrambled, abbreviated version of a real Latin text: Cicero's "De finibus bonorum et malorum" ("On the Ends of Good and Evil"), written in 45 BC. The passage that gives us "Lorem ipsum" comes from a section discussing the nature of pleasure and pain. Over the centuries the words were jumbled and truncated, the opening "Lorem" is actually a broken fragment of "dolorem", until the meaning was lost but the Latin-like texture remained.

It entered modern design in the 1960s when a company printed the text on sheets of Letraset transfer type, and it spread worldwide when early desktop-publishing software shipped it as sample text. That is why, decades later, it remains the default filler in design tools everywhere.

Why designers use placeholder text at all

The purpose of lorem ipsum is to remove meaning so you can focus on form. Real text is distracting: stakeholders read it, react to it and start editing copy when they are supposed to be reviewing a layout. Meaningless text keeps everyone's attention on spacing, hierarchy, line length and rhythm, the things a visual design is meant to solve.

There is also a practical reason the Latin looks right. Its distribution of word lengths and letter frequencies roughly matches English, so a paragraph of lorem ipsum takes up about the same space and creates a similar visual "colour" on the page as the real English copy will. Fill text made of repeated English words like "content content content" would misrepresent how the final page reads.

Lorem ipsum is a placeholder, not a decision

Filler text buys time, but it can also hide problems. A layout that looks great full of even, meaningless paragraphs may fall apart when the real copy arrives with long headings, short blurbs and awkward names. Test with realistic content before you finalise.

When you should not use lorem ipsum

Placeholder text is a tool, and like any tool it can be misused. Avoid it in these situations:

  • Client presentations where content matters. If the message is the point, dummy text makes the design feel unfinished and invites "what will it actually say?" questions.
  • Anything that could ship by accident. Real websites have gone live with "Lorem ipsum" still in the footer. If it can reach production, it eventually will.
  • Content-shaped components. For things like names, dates, prices and addresses, structured fake data looks far more realistic than Latin.
  • Accessibility and SEO testing. Meaningless text tells you nothing about heading structure or keyword flow.

Generating the right amount of filler

How much placeholder text you need depends on what you are testing. A hero section might need a single punchy sentence; a blog template needs several full paragraphs to reveal how long-form reading feels; a card grid needs many short blurbs of varying length. A good generator lets you choose the output by paragraphs, sentences or words so you get a realistic amount rather than one giant block.

From placeholder to polished

The best way to use lorem ipsum is as a scaffold you always intend to replace. Design the layout with filler to get the structure right, then swap in real content early enough to catch the surprises, the heading that wraps to three lines, the testimonial that is only four words, the product name nobody accounted for. When the real copy goes in, run it through the Word Counter to check it fits the space the placeholder implied.

Alternatives to classic lorem ipsum

Traditional lorem ipsum is not the only option, and sometimes not the best one. Because the fake Latin can look odd to clients and occasionally distracts more than it helps, designers have created themed placeholder texts: playful variants that read as coherent nonsense in English, industry-specific filler, and "greeking" that uses real sentences from public-domain literature. Each has a purpose. English-readable filler helps non-designers who find the Latin off-putting; themed filler can make a pitch feel more tailored.

There is a trade-off, though. The whole point of classic lorem ipsum is that it is meaningless, so no one reads it. The moment placeholder text becomes readable and amusing, people start reading it, and reacting to it, which defeats its job of keeping attention on the design. As a rule, use meaningless lorem ipsum when you want reviewers focused purely on layout, and switch to realistic or themed content only when you are testing how genuine copy will feel. And always replace all of it, whichever kind you choose, before anything ships.

If you remember one thing, make it this: placeholder text is a promise to yourself that real content is coming. The layouts that age worst are the ones where lorem ipsum quietly became permanent because no one circled back. Build a habit of marking every placeholder block clearly and replacing it on a schedule, and the fake Latin stays what it is meant to be, a temporary scaffold that helps you judge design, rather than an embarrassment discovered live on the site months later.

Understanding what lorem ipsum is, meaningless Latin that mimics the shape of real text, is what lets you use it well: as a way to judge design without being distracted by words, and never as a substitute for the content that actually has to arrive.
